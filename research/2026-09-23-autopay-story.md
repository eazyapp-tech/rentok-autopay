# Why a tenant would set up autopay: the story, walked as her (23 Sep 2026)

Sanchay, 23 Sep: the story is not about UPI charges. It is that autopay is easier and better, it
comes with RentOk rewards (Rent Coins, spent at the Hubble voucher store), and it is the first of
the recommended ways to pay, ahead of e-NACH and the virtual bank account. This file walks the
journey as a tenant and turns it into a story, copy and a visual direction. Late fines are taken
as known; no figures are needed to tell this story.

## The story in one line

**Rent that pays itself, and never a rupee more than your bill.**

Every autopay product sells ease. Rent adds a fear no other autopay has: *the landlord's hand in
my account.* The first half of the line is the reason to start. The second half is the reason she
trusts it, and it is true by design: the ceiling she approves (R68), the current month only (R69),
never a late fine (R29), and every debit announced two days before.

## Who she is

**Priya, 24, works in Gurugram, lives in a PG.** ₹12,000 rent plus ₹2,000 food. Salary lands
between the 1st and the 5th. Each month a WhatsApp link arrives from her PG and she pays by GPay,
usually a few days late, sometimes after a call from the manager. She has paid a late fine once
when her salary came late, and it still annoys her.

**Her job:** when rent is due, get it done without thinking about it or being chased, without
losing money to fines, and without handing anyone a blank cheque on her account.

**Why she would say no, in her words, and our answer:**

| She thinks | What we say, and why it is true |
| --- | --- |
| "What if they take more than I owe?" | It only ever takes your monthly bill, up to the limit you approve. Never old dues, never fines. (R68, R69, R29) |
| "What if I'm short that month?" | You get a message two days before with the exact amount, and a check-balance link. You can pay another way before it runs. |
| "What if it takes money after I move out?" | It ends with your stay. (R50: the mandate ends at agreement end; today eviction does not cancel the mandate, a known defect to fix before this is said.) |
| "I don't want to be locked in." | Change your day, pause or stop, anytime, from here or from your UPI app. (R45; pause wording waits on open question 30) |
| "Who is actually taking the money?" | Your bank shows [property name]. Approve only if it matches where you live. (Kamal's framing, open question 6) |
| "Is this safe?" | It runs through your own UPI app and your bank, with your PIN. RentOk never sees your PIN. |

## What the best products say

Read on Mobbin and from Kamal's CRED recording, 23 Sep.

- **CRED:** "automate your bills. never miss a due date." Then five cards: manage all bills
  effortlessly; never miss a due, never face a fine; support that doesn't keep you waiting; **you
  stay in control, always ("nothing moves without your say")**; built for safety, trusted by millions.
- **Apple Wallet:** one calm sentence. "Make payments automatically... You'll be notified before and
  after each payment is made." The reassurance is inside the promise.
- **Thrive Market (Autoship):** three pillars, exactly the shape we need. **Convenience, Savings,
  Control** ("we'll send reminders, and you can always edit, skip or cancel").
- **Zopa:** names the fear and answers it. "Recurring doesn't mean automatic... your money's safe,
  you're in total control."
- **Monzo:** humour a young renter recognises. "Never spend your rent money on pizza again."
- **Saily, Kraken:** the hero picture is the product's own object in its finished state, not a
  stock icon.
- **Cleo:** a visible "Pay manually" row under the autopay switch. The alternative is honest and
  quiet, not hidden.

**The pattern:** an outcome in the headline, then **ease, reward, control**, then one calm action,
with the manual way visible but quiet. Nobody leads with a penalty for not doing it.

## Our four pillars, in her words

1. **Handled.** Rent leaves on your day, by itself. No links, no reminders, nobody chasing you.
2. **Only your bill.** It takes exactly what's on your bill, never more. You see the amount two days
   before.
3. **Always yours.** Change your day, pause or stop, anytime.
4. **Rewarded.** Rent Coins every month you're on time, to spend on vouchers from brands you use.

Ease opens the story, trust carries it, reward closes it. Trust is the pillar no competitor has had
to answer for rent, so it gets the strongest visual treatment, not the smallest print.

## The journey, stage by stage

| Stage | Where she is | What she thinks | What she feels | What we show |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Notices** | A rent link on WhatsApp; the card on her bill; a moment right after she pays | "Another thing to sign up for?" | Busy, mildly wary | The card, with one line from her own record ("You usually pay on the 5th. It can pay itself.") |
| **Considers** | Taps the card, or "How it works" | "What's the catch?" | Curious, guarded | The four pillars, and the fears table answered in "How it works" (CRED's FAQ, in our words) |
| **Chooses** | What it pays, then her day | "Which one is safer?" | Deciding | Each option with its consequence in one line; only the days she may pick |
| **Checks** | The review | "Is anything hidden?" | Most anxious moment | Every term on one screen, "yours to pay now" on its own line, autopay charges ₹0 |
| **Approves** | Her UPI app | "Is this really my PG?" | Tense | Her app's button, the name her bank will show, her PIN |
| **Lands** | Back on the page | "Did it work?" | Relief, if we say so | "You're set" with her day on the calendar, the coins she earned, and what happens next |
| **First debit** | WhatsApp, two days before, then the receipt | "Right amount?" | Trust confirmed or broken | The exact amount, then "Paid by autopay" and coins |
| **Every month** | Heads-up, receipt, coins | Barely thinks about it | Calm | The same three messages, never more |
| **Something goes wrong** | A failed debit | "Will I be fined?" | Worried | Same-day message with the reason, Pay now, and the retry time |
| **Tells a flatmate** | Chatting in the PG | "Rent just happens now" | A little proud | A referral with coins, later |

**The moments that decide it:** the card tap (does the promise beat the fear), the review (does it
feel honest), the PIN (does the name match), and the first debit (was it exactly as promised). Lose
the first debit's trust and she cancels, which 437 tenants have already done in their UPI apps.

**The aha moment** is not the setup. It is the first month a "Rent paid" message arrives and she did
nothing.

## Copy, screen by screen

**The card on her bill**
- Title: **Rent that pays itself**
- Line (her own record, as today): "You usually pay on the 5th. It can pay itself."
- Button: **Set up autopay**. Quiet link: **How it works**

**Step 1, what it pays**
- Title: **What should autopay pay?**
- **All my monthly dues** · Recommended · "Rent, food and other monthly bills. Nothing to pay by hand."
- **Rent only** · "₹12,000 each month. You pay other bills yourself."

**Step 2, her day**
- Title: **When should it leave your account?**
- Line: "Any day from your rent date to the end of your grace days. Pick one after your salary lands."
- Only the allowed days, as a row of day cards.

**Step 3, review**
- Title: **Check and approve**
- The ledger: most in one debit, what it takes, first debit, yours to pay now, autopay charges
  ₹0 · free, heads-up two days before, if a debit fails, valid until, your bank will show.
- Above the button: "By approving, you let [property] collect your monthly dues through autopay as
  shown. Stop anytime."
- Button: **Approve in my UPI app**

**Step 4, approve**
- Title: **Approve with**
- GPay, PhonePe, Paytm, BHIM. "Someone else pays your rent? Send them a code." "Use bank account
  instead."

**Step 5, the outcome**
- Waiting: **Waiting for your bank.** "Some banks take a few minutes. Nothing leaves your account
  until they confirm."
- Done: **You're set.** "Rent pays itself on the 30th. We'll message you two days before." Coins
  earned.
- Didn't finish: **Your bank didn't get the approval.** "Your choices are saved. Try again, it takes
  a few seconds."

**Copy rules**
- Her words, never ours: "rent date", "grace days", "pay by hand". Never "mandate", "debit
  presentment", "NACH".
- Every promise must be true on the day it ships. "No late fines" is only said with its condition.
- The consequence of each choice is said before she makes it.
- Numbers are hers (her rent, her day, her ceiling), never examples.

## Pictures, icons and 3D

- **The product's own objects, never stock icons.** Sanchay ruled on 11 Sep that a stock bell or
  shield says nothing about her. We already have the glass calendar with her day on it and the
  autopay badge; extend that family rather than start another.
- **New objects in the same glass style:** a Rent Coin; her bill with "Paid by autopay" stamped on
  it; the WhatsApp "Rent due" reminder struck through (already on the card).
- **Where generated imagery earns its place:** the Rent Coin and the "You're set" moment, generated
  to match the calendar's material, then kept as fixed assets. The flow itself is built as real
  screens with her real numbers.
- **Motion:** the day turning over on the calendar when she picks it; coins landing on "You're set".
  Reduced motion turns both off.

## The recommended order of ways to pay

Autopay first, e-NACH second, then the virtual bank account, then everything else (Sanchay, 23 Sep).
Two of those carry open decisions: e-NACH as the default above ₹15,000 (open question 24), and the
virtual account parked until Cashfree answers on settlement and the aggregation warning is cleared
(open question 21).

## One line to hold on rewards

**Coins and vouchers are a reward. A lower rent for autopay users is a price difference by payment
method,** which our legal notes mark high risk: those who pay by link would in effect pay more, which
is the UPI charge reappearing by another route, and CCPA has been examining payment-method fees.
Rewards for setting up and for each on-time month are ordinary loyalty, common across Indian payment
apps. Recommended: say "rewards", never "discount on rent", and have the lawyer read the rewards line
with the agreement wording.
